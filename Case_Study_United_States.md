## The Research Question

The United States is the world's largest economy, a global military superpower, and the primary architect of the post-WWII international financial system. Its model — consumer-driven, innovation-led, and deeply financialized — has been the dominant template for global capitalism.

**The central question is:**

> *"Is the US model of financialized capitalism sustainable? What are its effects on human flourishing, ecological regeneration, and systemic resilience — both domestically and globally? And what does its trajectory reveal about the limits of perpetual growth?"*

---

## Historical Context

| Period | Event | Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1776** | Declaration of Independence; founding of the United States. | A nation built on settler colonialism, slavery, and vast natural resources. |
| **1865** | End of Civil War; abolition of slavery. | However, racial and economic inequality persist to the present day. |
| **1913** | Federal Reserve established. | Creation of a central bank, centralizing monetary policy. |
| **1929–1933** | Great Depression. | Massive economic collapse; leads to New Deal reforms. |
| **1944** | Bretton Woods Agreement. | US dollar becomes the world's reserve currency; IMF and World Bank established. |
| **1971** | Nixon ends gold convertibility. | End of Bretton Woods; transition to fully fiat monetary system. |
| **1980s** | Deregulation and financialization. | Reagan-era policies accelerate financial sector growth. |
| **2008** | Global Financial Crisis. | A systemic crisis rooted in financial speculation and deregulation. |
| **2020** | COVID-19 pandemic. | Massive stimulus; US deficit and debt reach historic levels. |
| **2025** | Ongoing challenges. | High inequality, political polarization, infrastructure decay, and ecological pressures. |

## Key Research Questions for Contributors

| Question | Why It Matters |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Is perpetual growth compatible with ecological limits?** | The US model relies on ever-increasing consumption. Is this sustainable given planetary boundaries? |
| **How does US financialization affect global stability?** | US financial markets and the dollar's reserve status transmit shocks globally. Is this a form of "spillover extraction"? |
| **What is the cost of US military and global hegemony?** | The US maintains a vast global military footprint. What is its economic, ecological, and social cost? |
| **Can the US transition to a regenerative economy?** | What would it take for the US to adopt a model based on regeneration, resilience, and renewal — not just growth? |
| **What does the US model reveal about the limits of capitalism itself?** | Does the US trajectory demonstrate that capitalism inevitably leads to concentration of wealth, ecological degradation, and social fragmentation? |
