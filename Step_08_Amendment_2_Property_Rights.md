### 9. Property Rights & Power Acknowledgement

**9.1 The Property Principle**

MEES operates **within** existing legal property regimes. It does not challenge, abolish, or replace national or local property law. All Microclimate experiments operate by consent of participants, not by expropriation.

The purpose of MEES is to **discover what works**—not to impose a new property system from above.

**9.2 Mandatory Disclosure of Power Structures**

Before launching a Microclimate, the founding stewards must publish a **Power & Property Disclosure** to the canonical record. This disclosure shall include:

| Item | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Land Ownership** | Who owns the land? (Private owners, community trust, state, indigenous title, etc.) |
| **Water Rights** | Who controls water access? (Permits, communal rights, riparian rights, etc.) |
| **Housing & Infrastructure** | Who owns the buildings, infrastructure, and equipment used in the experiment? |
| **Economic Power** | Which actors hold significant economic power (large employers, major landlords, debt-holders) within or adjacent to the Microclimate? |
| **Legal Constraints** | What legal constraints exist (zoning laws, contract obligations, regulatory frameworks) that affect the experiment? |
| **Historical Context** | How was land and property acquired (colonization, land reform, inheritance, purchase)? |

**The disclosure is not a judgment.** It is a **transparency requirement**—so that participants and observers understand the baseline context in which the experiment operates.

**9.3 Consent and Non-Expropriation**

- All participants must explicitly consent to the Microclimate's operation within the existing property regime.
- No participant is required to give up land, water, or property rights to participate.
- The experiment operates by **voluntary participation**, not by coercion or expropriation.
- The Regenerative Ledger tracks flows and impacts **within** the existing property structure—it does not replace it.

**9.4 Power-Sensitive Design**

Where pre-existing power imbalances are significant (e.g., large landowners, powerful employers), the Microclimate design shall include:

- **Weighted participation** (ensuring that voices of less powerful participants are heard, e.g., through random selection for councils, not just self-selection).
- **Transparent tracking** of how power-holders interact with the experiment (e.g., large employers' compliance with regenerative practices).
- **Renewal Council safeguards** (ensuring that no single power-holder can dominate the Renewal Council, e.g., maximum one representative per entity).

**9.5 The "Colonial Framing" Self-Critique**

MEES explicitly acknowledges that the term "stewardship" and the very concept of a constitutional framework may carry colonial or Western-centric connotations. To address this:

- MEES welcomes and actively seeks participation from indigenous, traditional, and non-Western economic systems.
- Any Microclimate located on indigenous land must obtain the **free, prior, and informed consent** of the relevant indigenous authorities.
- The framework is designed to be **adapted** to local cultural, legal, and economic contexts—not imposed as a universal blueprint.

**9.6 The Limits of MEES**

MEES does **not** claim to solve systemic injustice (colonial land theft, structural racism, historical inequality). It aims to:
- **Make visible** the effects of economic activity on health and regeneration.
- **Provide tools** for communities to transition toward regenerative practices.
- **Generate evidence** that can inform policy and social change.

MEES is a **diagnostic and experimental tool**, not a political revolution. Participants may use the evidence it generates to advocate for broader change—but the experiment itself does not enforce change beyond its voluntary boundaries.

---

### 2. Rationale for This Amendment

Kimi correctly identified that MEES risks becoming a "technocratic overlay on fundamentally extractive property relations." By:

- **Acknowledging** the existence of property regimes (rather than pretending they don't exist),
- **Requiring disclosure** of power structures (so participants know the context),
- **Ensuring voluntary consent** (no expropriation),
- **Designing for power sensitivity** (weighted participation, safeguards),
- **Self-critiquing** its own framing (colonial connotations, seeking indigenous input),
- **Acknowledging its limits** (it is a tool, not a revolution),

MEES becomes a **tool that communities can use within existing realities**—not a blueprint that ignores them. This makes the framework more honest, more adaptable, and more resilient to real-world complexity.
