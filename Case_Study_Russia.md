## The Research Question

Russia is a nation of paradoxes. It possesses vast natural resources, a highly educated population, and a central role in global energy markets. Yet its economy remains heavily dependent on oil, gas, and mineral extraction — a classic case of the "resource curse" at the national scale.

**The central question is:**

> *"Is resource dependency inevitably a path to fragility? What happens when fossil fuel demand declines, and what lessons does Russia offer for nations seeking to escape the trap of extraction?"*

---

## Historical Context

| Period | Event | Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1917** | Bolshevik Revolution. | Establishment of the Soviet Union; command economy. |
| **1991** | Collapse of the Soviet Union. | Transition to market economy; massive economic dislocation. |
| **1990s** | Oligarchic capitalism. | Rapid privatization; extreme inequality; economic collapse. |
| **2000s** | Putin era; oil boom. | Rising oil prices fuel economic growth; state reasserts control over strategic industries. |
| **2014** | Crimea annexation; sanctions. | International sanctions impose significant costs. |
| **2022** | Invasion of Ukraine; intensified sanctions. | Sanctions deepen; gas exports to Europe disrupted. |
| **2023–2025** | War economy; Chinese pivot. | Military spending drives growth; dependence on China deepens; structural fragility intensifies. |
