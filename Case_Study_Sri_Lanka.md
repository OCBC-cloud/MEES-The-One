## The Research Question

Sri Lanka (formerly Ceylon) was a prized Dutch colony for over 150 years. Its cinnamon monopoly was one of the most lucrative trade assets of the Dutch East India Company (VOC). The Dutch established a plantation economy that would later be intensified by the British, leaving a legacy of ethnic division, export dependency, and recurring economic crises.

**The central question is:**

> *"How did the Dutch colonial extraction model shape Sri Lanka's economic and social structures, and what lessons does its history offer for understanding the long-term effects of commodity dependency, ethnic fragmentation, and institutional fragility?"*

---

## Historical Context

| Period | Event | Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1640** | Dutch capture of Galle, Ceylon, from the Portuguese. | Beginning of Dutch colonial rule in Ceylon. |
| **1658** | Complete Dutch control of Ceylon. | Dutch East India Company (VOC) establishes a monopoly over cinnamon and other spices. |
| **17th–18th Century** | Dutch plantation economy | Cinnamon, spices, and later coffee and sugar, cultivated under a forced labour system. |
| **1796** | British capture of Ceylon from the Dutch. | Beginning of British colonial rule (formalized in 1815). |
| **1802** | British Crown Colony of Ceylon. | Coffee and later tea plantations expand dramatically. |
| **1948** | Independence from Britain. | Ceylon becomes independent; Dutch colonial period largely forgotten in the national narrative. |
| **1972** | Ceylon becomes the Republic of Sri Lanka. | Shift in national identity, but ethnic tensions persist. |
| **1983–2009** | Sri Lankan Civil War. | Ethnic conflict between Sinhalese majority and Tamil minority. |
| **2022** | Economic collapse. | Sri Lanka defaults on its debt; food, fuel, and medicine shortages; political crisis. |
| **2023–2024** | IMF bailout, partial recovery. | Ongoing economic fragility, political instability, and unresolved ethnic tensions. |
