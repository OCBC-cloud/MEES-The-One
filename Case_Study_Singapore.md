## The Research Question

Singapore is a city-state without natural resources, without a hinterland, and without a large domestic market. Yet it has achieved one of the highest GDP per capita figures in the world — often ranking first in purchasing power parity (PPP) terms.

**The central question is:**

> *"How did a tiny, resource-poor city-state achieve such extraordinary prosperity, and what lessons does its model offer for understanding the relationship between governance, globalization, and economic health? Is the Singapore model replicable, or does it depend on unique historical and geopolitical circumstances?"*

---

## Historical Context

| Period | Event | Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1819** | Sir Stamford Raffles establishes a British trading post. | Beginning of modern Singapore; strategic location. |
| **1826** | Singapore becomes part of the Straits Settlements. | British colonial administration; free port policy. |
| **1942–1945** | Japanese occupation. | Brief but brutal period; exposes British vulnerability. |
| **1963** | Merger with Malaysia. | Short-lived; political and economic tensions. |
| **1965** | Separation from Malaysia; independence. | A reluctant independence; no natural resources, no hinterland. |
| **1965–1990** | Lee Kuan Yew's leadership. | State-led industrialization, foreign investment attraction, housing, education, and public health reforms. |
| **1990s–2000s** | Regional hub. | Financial services, logistics, and high-tech manufacturing expand. |
| **2010s–2020s** | Global city-state. | Continued leadership in finance, technology, and as a global business hub. |

## Key Research Questions for Contributors

| Question | Why It Matters |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **How did Singapore achieve such extraordinary prosperity with no resources?** | Singapore's success is often attributed to governance, education, and globalization. What specific policies enabled this? |
| **Is the Singapore model replicable?** | Singapore's unique geopolitical position (post-colonial British hub, Cold War strategic importance) may not be replicable. What are the limits of the "city-state" model? |
| **What is the cost of Singapore's success?** | High inequality, reliance on foreign labor, and limited political participation are often cited as costs. What does the MEES Health Architecture reveal about these trade-offs? |
| **How does Singapore manage ecological resilience?** | Singapore is highly dependent on imports for water, energy, and food. How does it manage these vulnerabilities? |
| **What would a MEES-style Renewal Mechanism propose for Singapore?** | How would a constitutional framework address inequality, foreign dependency, and long-term sustainability? |
