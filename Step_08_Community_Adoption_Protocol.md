## MEES - The One
### Step 08: Community Adoption Protocol (Voluntary Use)
**Status:** CONSTITUTIONAL PROPOSAL  
**Purpose:** To describe how a community might voluntarily adopt the MEES framework—should it choose to do so. MEES does not solicit, recruit, or manage such adoptions.

---

### 1. The Adoption Principle

MEES is a constitutional framework, not a program. Communities that find the framework compelling may choose to adopt it. MEES has no role in their adoption other than providing the canonical record.

### 2. Adoption Conditions

If a community chooses to adopt MEES, it must:
- Agree to the Constitution (Step 01) as a guiding document.
- Operate transparently (publish its own adoption record).
- Accept that MEES provides no funding, staff, or enforcement.

### 3. MEES's Role

MEES does not:
- Initiate adoptions.
- Recruit participants.
- Provide technical support.
- Verify compliance.

MEES exists solely as a public repository of constitutional ideas. Its integrity depends on the quality of its reasoning, not the number of adoptions.

### 4. Relationship to the Commentary Period

The only formal process involving external parties is the 90-day commentary period (Step 00). Communities, scholars, and citizens may contribute challenges, endorsements, or proposals—exactly as the Open Participation Protocol (Step 10) describes.

---

### 5. Jurisdictional Compatibility Principle

MEES adoption does not itself alter, extinguish, suspend, transfer, or subordinate any legal obligation unless the relevant parties and competent legal authority have separately provided lawful effect.

Every adopting community must identify which MEES mechanisms are legally operative, contractually voluntary, or purely informational within its jurisdiction. This prevents the dangerous misunderstanding that adoption equals legal force.
