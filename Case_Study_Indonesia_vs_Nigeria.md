## The Research Question

Indonesia and Nigeria are two of the largest economies in their respective regions. Both are former colonies, both are rich in natural resources, and both have large, diverse populations.

**Yet their trajectories have diverged significantly:**

- **Indonesia** has achieved sustained growth, relative political stability, and a gradual transition toward diversified industrialization.
- **Nigeria** has struggled with political instability, corruption, and a persistent resource curse, despite enormous oil wealth.

**The central question is:**

> *"Why have Indonesia and Nigeria — two comparable post-colonial nations — followed such different development paths, and what lessons does this offer for understanding the role of governance, institutional design, and economic policy in shaping national health and resilience?"*

---

## Historical Context

| Period | 🇮🇩 Indonesia | 🇳🇬 Nigeria |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Colonial Period** | Dutch East Indies (1800–1949). Extractive economy: spices, rubber, oil, and forced labor. | British Nigeria (1861–1960). Extractive economy: palm oil, cocoa, tin, and later oil. |
| **Independence** | 1945 (declared), 1949 (recognized). | 1960 (from Britain). |
| **Post-Independence** | Sukarno's guided democracy (1950s–1960s); Suharto's New Order (1966–1998) — authoritarian but development-focused. | Civil War (1967–1970); military coups; corruption and instability dominate. |
| **1990s–2000s** | Transition to democracy (1998–2004); rapid economic growth; industrialization. | Return to civilian rule (1999); persistent corruption, insurgency (Boko Haram), and oil dependency. |
| **2010s–2020s** | Continued growth; investment in infrastructure, manufacturing, and technology. | Stagnation; oil dependency; rising debt; political and security challenges. |
