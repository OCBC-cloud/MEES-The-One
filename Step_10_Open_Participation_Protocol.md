## MEES - The One
### Step 10: Open Participation Protocol (GOVERNANCE MECHANISM)
**Status:** DRAFT — Crowd-Generative Governance Design  
**Purpose:** Define the mechanism by which any human can contribute to MEES — submit evidence, challenge claims, propose amendments, and participate in governance — without requiring credentials, institutional affiliation, or ideological conformity.

---

### 1. The Participation Principle

MEES is **open to all humanity** — not as observers, but as contributors.

Participation is not conditional on:
- Academic credentials
- Institutional endorsement
- Ideological alignment
- Geographic location
- Economic status

**The only condition:** Contributions must be accompanied by **provenance** (source, timestamp, methodology) and must be open to **challenge**.

This is the operational expression of Constitutional Articles I and II.

---

### 2. Participation Pathways

| Pathway | Description | Access |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Observation** | Access the public dashboard and read the canonical record | Open to all |
| **Challenge** | Submit a challenge to any data point, claim, or proposal | Open to all (with provenance) |
| **Proposal** | Submit a new idea, amendment, or experimental design | Open to all (with rationale) |
| **Verification** | Participate in data verification and auditing | Open to all (training provided) |
| **Governance** | Participate in assemblies, councils, and voting | Open to all participants in a Microclimate |
| **Stewardship** | Serve as SDS steward (global governance) | By election/appointment from participant cohort |

---

### 3. Challenge Protocol

**Any participant may challenge:**
- A data point in the Regenerative Ledger (e.g., "This soil health score is inaccurate")
- A claim in an AI debate transcript (e.g., "The AI's analysis missed an alternative explanation")
- A proposal or amendment (e.g., "This proposed change would violate the Constitution")
- A decision by a local steward or Renewal Council (e.g., "This credit allocation was unfair")

**Process:**
1. **Submit challenge** via public form or API.
2. **Providence required:** Source, evidence, methodology.
3. **AI Orchestrator** receives the challenge and convenes an AI debate on it.
4. **Public transcript** of the AI debate is published.
5. **Human review:** Local stewards or Renewal Council (as appropriate) review the challenge and the AI debate.
6. **Decision** is published, with rationale.
7. **If challenge is upheld:** The data point is flagged, corrected (with audit trail), or the decision is reversed.

**Appeal:** Any participant may appeal a decision to the next higher governance level (local → SDS → global participant vote).

---

### 4. Proposal Protocol

**Any participant may submit a proposal for:**
- A constitutional amendment
- A new experimental design (Microclimate)
- A modification to the Renewal Mechanism
- A change to the Health Architecture (new indicators, weighting adjustments)
- A change to the Open Participation Protocol itself

**Process:**
1. **Submit proposal** via public form or API.
2. **Providence required:** Rationale, evidence, expected impact, and a "What if this fails?" section.
3. **AI Orchestrator** receives the proposal and convenes an AI debate on it (pro/con analysis).
4. **Public transcript** of the AI debate is published.
5. **Minimum 90-day commentary period** (Constitutional Article VI) — open to all participants.
6. **Vote:** If a constitutional amendment, supermajority (≥⅔) of active participants required.
7. **If approved:** The proposal is absorbed into The One, with a transparent evolution record.
8. **If rejected:** The proposal is archived with rationale. The proposer may resubmit with new evidence.

---

### 5. Verification Protocol

**Any participant may volunteer as a verifier:**
- Data point verification (e.g., "I will go to the site and confirm the soil health measurement")
- Ledger audit (e.g., "I will review all transactions for accuracy")
- AI debate quality check (e.g., "I will review the transcript and flag potential hallucination")

**Process:**
1. **Sign up** as a verifier (no credentials required).
2. **Training provided:** A simple, online guide to MEES verification methodology.
3. **Assign verification tasks** (based on availability and interest).
4. **Complete verification** and submit a report.
5. **Report is published** alongside the original data point (with the verifier's name or pseudonym).
6. **Quality check:** Any participant may challenge a verification report.

---

### 6. Governance Participation

**Local Governance:**
- **Participant Assembly:** All participants in a Microclimate may attend and vote on local matters.
- **Local Stewards:** Elected by the Participant Assembly.
- **Renewal Council:** Randomly selected from participants (no self-nomination).

**Global Governance (SDS):**
- **SDS Stewards:** Elected by a global vote of all active Microclimate participants.
- **Minimum term:** 2 years.
- **Maximum term:** 3 terms (6 years).
- **Recall:** A supermajority (≥⅔) of global participants may recall an SDS steward at any time.

**Voting Mechanics:**
- One person, one vote (regardless of contribution level).
- Voting is open to all active participants (defined as having participated in at least one survey or transaction in the last 12 months).
- Votes are recorded in the canonical record.
- Voting results are published with anonymized participation counts.

---

### 7. The "Zero Credential" Principle

No contribution is rejected based on the contributor's credentials.

**However:**
- Contributions without provenance are flagged as "unverified."
- Unverified contributions are not used in formal experimental design (Constitutional Article II, 2.2).
- But they are **not deleted** — they remain visible, and other participants may add provenance.

This means:
- A farmer with no academic degree can contribute soil health data — and it will be taken seriously if properly documented.
- A student can challenge an AI claim — and if the challenge holds, it will change the record.
- A citizen in a developing country can propose a measurement adaptation — and it will enter the global conversation.

**This is the core of "Let all contribute. Let evidence decide."**

---

### 8. Protections Against Capture

| Threat | Mitigation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Spam contributions** | Rate limiting; provenance requirement; AI flagging of low-quality submissions |
| **Coordinated voting manipulation** | Voter verification (unique human identity); public voting records; anomaly detection by AI |
| **Steward capture by vested interests** | Term limits; recall mechanism; public financial disclosure; AI monitoring of stewards' decisions |
| **AI manipulation by prompt injection** | Standardized prompts; cross-model comparison; public transparency of all prompts |
| **Statistical manipulation** | Independent verification layer; any participant may audit; public data transparency |

---

### 9. Incentives for Participation

| Activity | Incentive |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Contributing data** | Visibility in the ledger; access to aggregated insights |
| **Challenging a claim** | Public recognition; influence on the record |
| **Submitting a proposal** | If adopted, credit as a contributor to The One |
| **Verification work** | Priority access to training; public recognition |
| **Governance participation** | Agency over the system |
| **Renewal Council service** | Respect; protected status during service |

**No financial incentives:** MEES is non-commercial. Participation is driven by agency, contribution, and collective benefit — not payment.

---

### 10. Connection to the System Model

The Open Participation Protocol operationalizes **Layer 3 (Decision)** of the System Model:

- **Layer 1 (Observation):** Data is collected (anyone can contribute).
- **Layer 2 (Challenge):** AI Orchestrator debates (anyone can challenge).
- **Layer 3 (Decision):** Humans decide (anyone can participate in governance).
- **Layer 4 (Action):** Action is taken (anyone can verify and contribute to renewal).
