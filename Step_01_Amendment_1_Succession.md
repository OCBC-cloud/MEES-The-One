### Amendment 1 to Step 01: SDS Succession & Continuity Protocol
**Status:** RATIFIED (Pending formal vote, adopted provisionally by Founding Steward)  
**Effective Date:** Immediately  
**Purpose:** To close the succession gap identified in the Kimi Red-Team review by establishing clear, binding rules for the continuity, incapacitation, resignation, or death of SDS Stewards.

### 1. Amendment to Article IV (Stewardship as Trust, Not Control)

The following new section is added to Article IV, replacing the existing language regarding SDS continuity with a specific, binding protocol.

**4.4 Succession, Incapacitation, and Continuity**

**4.4.1 The Stewardship Mandate**

SDS is a constitutional role, not a personal property. Its authority is derived from the Constitution, not from the individual holding it. Therefore, the continuity of SDS must be ensured regardless of the circumstances of any single Steward.

**4.4.2 Incapacitation or Temporary Unavailability**

If a Steward is temporarily unavailable (illness, travel, force majeure) for less than 30 consecutive days, the remaining Stewards (if any) shall manage the canonical record. If the Steward is the sole Steward, all non-urgent governance decisions are paused. The Dead-Man's Switch (Article III.3) remains active but escalates to a pre-designated emergency contact.

**4.4.3 Resignation, Permanent Incapacitation, or Death**

If a Steward resigns, becomes permanently incapacitated, or passes away, the following sequence shall be executed:

1.  **Activation (Day 1):** A public notice is issued to the canonical record and the Open Participation Protocol.
2.  **Interim Stewardship (Days 1–30):** The five (5) longest-serving active local stewards from any existing Microclimate shall be notified to convene. These five individuals shall collectively serve as the **Interim Stewardship Council (ISC)** .
3.  **ISC Powers:** The ISC shall have limited powers:
    - Maintain the canonical record (approve or reject commits to the Constitution).
    - Manage the Dead-Man's Switch escalation process.
    - **Forbidden:** The ISC shall not ratify constitutional amendments or initiate new Microclimates.
4.  **Succession Nomination (Days 15–45):** The ISC shall publish a call for candidates for the permanent SDS Steward role. Candidates must be active MEES participants with a proven record of contribution and must publicly accept the constitutional constraints of Article IV.
5.  **Global Vote (Days 45–75):** A binding global vote shall be held. All active participants (defined as those who have contributed to a Microclimate within the last 12 months) are eligible to vote. The candidate must achieve a supermajority (≥⅔) of the votes cast.
6.  **Handover (Day 90):** The ISC hands over all constitutional keys and access rights to the newly elected Steward. The handover is recorded in the canonical record with cryptographic signatures.

**4.4.4 Protection Against "Hostile Takeover"**

To prevent a bad actor from exploiting the succession protocol:

- Any candidate found to have materially misrepresented their participation history shall be immediately disqualified.
- The ISC shall be randomly selected from the pool of eligible stewards, ensuring no single Microclimate can dominate the process.
- All candidate nominations, voter registrations, and final tallies are published to the immutable public log.

**4.4.5 Emergency Archival**

In the absolute worst-case scenario where no ISC can be formed (e.g., no Microclimates exist yet, and the Founding Steward is permanently unavailable), the following default shall apply:

- The canonical record (GitHub repository) shall be archived as a historical artifact.
- **No further changes** shall be made to the Constitution or The One.
- The project shall be considered **indefinitely paused** until a new human comes forward to claim stewardship with proof of the Founding Steward's original mandate.

This amendment is a direct response to the Kimi Red-Team review, which correctly identified that without a succession plan, SDS is a single point of failure. By embedding this protocol, MEES becomes anti-fragile—capable of surviving the loss of any single participant, including its founder.
