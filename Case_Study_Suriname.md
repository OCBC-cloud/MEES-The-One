## The Research Question

Suriname is a nation of paradoxes. It was one of the most lucrative Dutch colonies, built on sugar, coffee, and slave labor. It received one of the largest independence compensation packages in history — **€6.5 billion** from the Netherlands in 1975. Yet today, it remains economically fragile, heavily dependent on resource extraction (gold, oil, bauxite), and vulnerable to external shocks.

**The central question is:**

> *"Why did Suriname, with its resource wealth and substantial independence settlement, fail to achieve sustained prosperity, resilience, and flourishing — and what can MEES learn from its trajectory?"*

---

## Historical Context

| Period | Event | Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1667** | Treaty of Breda | Dutch acquire Suriname from England in exchange for New Netherland (now New York). |
| **17th–19th Century** | Plantation economy | Sugar, coffee, cocoa, and cotton plantations powered by enslaved Africans. |
| **1863** | Abolition of slavery | Enslaved people emancipated, but economic structures remain largely unchanged. |
| **1948** | Autonomy | Suriname becomes a constituent country within the Kingdom of the Netherlands. |
| **1975** | Independence | Netherlands grants independence; Suriname receives **€6.5 billion** in development aid. |
| **1980** | Military coup | Dictatorship under Desi Bouterse; Dutch aid suspended. |
| **1980s–1990s** | Civil war | Internal conflict devastates the interior. |
| **2000s** | Resource boom | Gold and oil extraction become dominant economic drivers. |
| **2020** | Debt crisis | Suriname defaults on international debt; IMF bailout required. |
| **2023–2024** | Oil discovery | Major offshore oil discoveries promising future revenue. |
